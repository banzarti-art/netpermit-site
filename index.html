For Android

Your phone. Your rules.

Most apps can use the Internet whenever they want, without asking you. NetPermit puts a switch beside every one of them. It runs on your phone, with no account, no ads and no tracking.

Coming soon to Google Play.

You decide

  • One switch for every app

    Your apps start blocked. You open the door only for the ones you trust, and close it again whenever you like.

  • New apps wait for you

    An app you install waits for your answer before it can connect. NetPermit tells you the moment it arrives.

  • See who's been knocking

    When a blocked app tries to connect anyway, NetPermit shows you the site it asked for and how many times it tried.

  • Even the parts you never see

    Your phone's background services start allowed, so calls, notifications and updates keep working. One tap blocks the ones it doesn't need.

  • Honest with you

    NetPermit says an app is blocked only once Android has confirmed it. If Android is overriding your choice, it tells you. No green tick it hasn't earned.

  • Private, because it never leaves

    No account, no ads, no analytics. Your rules and your activity stay on your phone.

How it works

NetPermit uses Android's VPN feature to do the blocking, so Android shows a key icon and calls it a VPN. It is a firewall that runs on your phone, not a VPN service. Your traffic is not sent to a remote server and your IP address does not change. Apps you block have their connection attempts discarded on the phone. Apps you allow connect directly, exactly as they would without NetPermit, and NetPermit never sees what they send.

Good to know

  • Android allows one VPN at a time, so NetPermit cannot run alongside another VPN app.
  • If Android's "Block connections without VPN" setting is switched on, Android blocks the apps you allow as well. NetPermit notices and tells you how to turn it off.
  • Site names appear in blocked activity when Android's Private DNS is on Automatic or Off. If it is set to a specific provider, those lookups are encrypted and only addresses can be shown.
  • A Wi-Fi-only app can use a little mobile data in the moment your phone switches from Wi-Fi to mobile data, before the block takes hold.
  • Blocking holds while NetPermit is on. While it is paused or switched off, nothing is blocked, and it says so.
  • NetPermit is not an antivirus, an ad blocker, a cleaner, or a way to monitor someone else's phone.
