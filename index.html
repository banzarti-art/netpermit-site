For Android

Your phone. Your rules.

NetPermit lets you choose which apps can use the Internet. It runs on your phone, with no account, no ads and no tracking.

Coming soon to Google Play.

What it does

  • One switch per app

    Your apps start blocked. Turn on the ones you want, and block them again whenever you like.

  • New apps wait for you

    An app you install starts blocked, and NetPermit tells you so you can decide.

  • See what they reach for

    When a blocked app tries to connect, NetPermit shows the site it asked for and how often.

  • Your phone keeps working

    The phone's own background parts start allowed, so calls, notifications and updates carry on. One tap blocks the ones it doesn't need.

  • Honest about what it knows

    NetPermit says an app is blocked or allowed only once Android has confirmed it. If Android is overriding your choice, it tells you.

  • Private by design

    No account, no ads, no analytics. Nothing about your apps or your activity leaves your phone.

How it works

NetPermit uses Android's VPN feature to do the blocking, so Android shows a key icon and calls it a VPN. It is a firewall that runs on your phone, not a VPN service. Your traffic is not sent to a remote server and your IP address does not change. Apps you block have their connection attempts discarded on the phone. Apps you allow connect directly, exactly as they would without NetPermit, and NetPermit never sees what they send.

Good to know

  • Android allows one VPN at a time, so NetPermit cannot run alongside another VPN app.
  • If Android's "Block connections without VPN" setting is switched on, Android blocks the apps you allow as well. NetPermit notices and tells you how to turn it off.
  • Site names appear in blocked activity when Android's Private DNS is on Automatic or Off. If it is set to a specific provider, those lookups are encrypted and only addresses can be shown.
  • A Wi-Fi-only app can use a little mobile data in the moment your phone switches from Wi-Fi to mobile data, before the block takes hold.
  • NetPermit is not an antivirus, a cleaner, or a way to monitor someone else's phone.
